01 / The field
In one sentence

Machine-readable brand specifications solve different problems. BRAND.md is a single readable brand file, the Brand Context Protocol publishes and discovers brand packages, MRBS structures a brand identity system, and OBDS decides which governed truth applies to a task and whether it may run.

Different problems, not better and worse.

This page exists so the category can be classified correctly. It is written by the OBDS publisher, so treat the OBDS row as self-description and check the others against their own primary sources, which are linked on every claim.

02 / Choosing

Which one is right depends on what breaks.

Choose OBDS when

  • Brand truth varies by scope or time, and the same question has different correct answers per market, channel or date.
  • Approved declarations can conflict, and which one wins has to be decided rather than argued.
  • Required truth may be missing, and you need that to be a state rather than a blank.
  • Execution must stop rather than guess, and a plausible sentence is worse than no sentence.
  • You need reproducible decision evidence: why this output was released, against which approved artefact, provable later.

OBDS may be unnecessary when

If you only need a lightweight AI-readable brand context, a simpler format may be more appropriate. If one file of tone, colours and do-and-do-not gets your agents on brand, use one file. If your problem is that tools cannot find your brand context at all, a discovery protocol solves that directly and OBDS does not solve it. Adopting a governance layer for a problem you do not have costs authoring effort, review effort and time, and returns nothing.

These approaches are not mutually exclusive. A readable brand file and a governed applicability layer answer different questions and can sit in the same stack.

What we are not claiming

OBDS is technically mature and publicly young. It is not an industry standard, not a de facto standard, not widely adopted and not certified, and there is no certification programme. Nothing on this page should be read as a ranking. We have not measured adoption of any project here, including our own, and we do not make adoption claims about others.
